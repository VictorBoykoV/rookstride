# Rookstride web experience

The website starts with a beginner's goal: rent a server, make it their own VPN,
connect supported devices and decide who can use it. Rook acts as a pathfinder in
an optional interactive explanation. Get started groups the actual ways to begin.
The deployment pipeline remains the product; its interfaces have equal standing,
with different current availability. This file supersedes the graphite/portrait
experiments; those dated reports remain historical evidence.

## Visual language

Canonical light canvas `#F5F6F8`, white surfaces, charcoal text `#141418`, muted
text `#5C616B`, red identity `#EE3038`, readable action red `#BF2637`, focus
`#8D2032`. Speech, availability, people and assistant icons use neutral surfaces.
Original Rook wardrobe/prop colors remain part of the character, not a separate
schematic palette.
Self-hosted Geist Sans and Mono remain; font license is in public/licenses.
Use full understandable sentences, readable text and 44–50px controls. Body text
carries the meaning; small scene labels are supplementary. No black site theme,
fake usage totals, typing terminal, mandatory animation or generic slogan strip.

## Graph and accent contract

The product motto is Your gateway. Keep moving. The expanded tagline is Deploy
your VPN. Share access. Keep moving. Rook's character line is separate. Every
selected route leg uses the same red role; the proposed route is neutral/dashed.
Two-server topology is device→entry→exit→internet, without an entry uplink shortcut.
An entry can be reachable near the user, including their own country; geography
alone does not establish access. Both connections and the chosen methods matter.
Two rentals and maintenance are explicit. Rented machines and their software roles
are distinct, and onward website encryption is not inferred from a colored line.
Check actual label/connector bounds at 320/390/640/1440px across both route modes.

## Provider audience and assistant identity

`/providers` is a prospective integration page reached from the footer. Guides,
first-boot adapters and reviewed marketplace listings are distinct proposals.
Its pilot brief can be copied or manually selected; nothing is submitted/sent.
One-server packaging/adoption is the proposed first pilot; two-resource orchestration
requires its own identity/routing/DNS/outage/resource tests. No provider logo wall,
relationship claim, working deploy badge or unqualified marketplace artifact.

Cursor/Gemini CLI use supplied unmodified marks with provenance in
`public/licenses/assistant-marks.txt`; they retain third-party trademark rights.
Codex/Claude use their names, replacing the invented symbols. A narrow neutral
vendor-mark exception does not introduce a new Rookstride accent palette.

## Ownership and connected visual refinement — decision088

The provider proposition centres on free ongoing supported VPN administration from
a phone: protocol/settings management, estimated usage, selected-user sharing and
revocation. Infrastructure is rented and upgraded with the provider. A free-app
recommendation plus guide is a useful partnership on its own. First-boot/catalog
packaging is optional. Potential retention/upgrade demand is not measured revenue.
The app does not implement provider resizing or whole-OS administration.

## Current route and browser experience, 18 November 2026

The light homepage has one primary action, “Set up your VPN.” The price line
explains that visitors pay a hosting provider directly for their server.
Rental guidance is available on the setup page. The
interactive guide shows two paths between the same device and destination. Red and
arrowheads identify the VPN, a stop symbol marks the possible block, and a dotted
continuation shows the direct request's intended destination. Labels and the
point explanations give the marks a text equivalent. A domestic entry and foreign
exit illustrate one placement. Ordinary language describes personal access, the
provider's role, estimated traffic and DNS filtering.

The browser path opens a personal single-server IKEv2 workspace with inspection,
approval, progress, guest controls and profile downloads. An independently
labelled practice area uses sample state. Both follow the same typography,
focus behavior and red/white/charcoal palette. The prospective provider page
separates owner, guest and host responsibilities and defines a testable pilot.

Select a device, server, direct-route stop or destination on the drawing to
open its explanation. Keep icon and label inside the same hit area in both
layouts. Use live HTML for labels, set in the portal typeface at 0.9375rem
with 0.8125rem supporting text. Only the connector geometry scales. The
device chooser changes the depicted hardware. The destination
shows original symbols for messaging, calling and reading. Guest access, usage
estimates and AdGuard filtering pair an icon with a visible heading.
