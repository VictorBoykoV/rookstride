# Rook’s connected worlds

**Five worlds · 50 complete chapters · 200 narrated story pages**

Rook steps from an atlas margin into Lantern Harbor, then travels as a guest
through four connected, culturally inspired worlds. His friends lend him
introductions, tools, and questions. He returns home with an atlas of invitations
that leaves room for the next traveler.

The complete English story collection is in `content/worlds.json`. Each chapter
has a summary, a theme, and four individually narrated pages with scene
descriptions and an art focus. The first five Lantern Harbor chapters retain
`legacyChapter: 1` through `5`, so their original six-panel comics remain
available. Their new four-page narration is a separate retelling of the same arc.

These are invented places with individual hosts, contemporary lives, and their
own decisions. Cultural motifs give readers a reason to become curious about
real communities. Primary-source notes provide a clear place to continue that
curiosity. The story’s travel order and geography are fictional.

## The recurring promise

**A good route leaves the next traveler a choice.**

Rook helps by asking, listening, checking, and sharing credit. He may be wrong or
impatient, and he puts things right when he can. His lantern is ordinary brass.
It needs care and lights only a small area. The book’s blank page invites another
voice; it does not fill itself with answers.

The princess is an astronomer with a plan. Dragons have names, needs, and choices;
a dragon who oversteps a public role can apologize and change it. The people
Rook meets participate in their own solutions. Each chapter finishes its central
adventure, and the reader decides when to begin another.

Supporting residents wear everyday clothes suited to the scene. A world’s

symbols are required for recognition.

## Narration and scene focus

Every chapter contains four complete pages, each **57–71 English words**. The
collection contains **12,879 words of page narration**, plus summaries, lore,
and captions. Each page has its own scene description rather than relying on
text baked into an illustration.

`focus` selects the illustrated element to emphasize during playback: Rook,
lantern, bridge, book, tower, dragon, friend, or route. `setting` selects a
background vocabulary: harbor, garden, desert, market, tower, river, mountain,
snow, library, rail, sea, or sky. These fields support reader presentation;
they do not introduce extra dialogue, facts, or product promises.

The words are written as an accessible original story. Narration should start
only after a reader presses Play, advance within the current chapter, and stop
at its ending. A reader chooses whether to start the next chapter. Captions and
a text edition remain useful without audio or animation.

## Cultural notes and real sources

`content/cultural-sources.json` records titles, organizations, URLs, checked date,
the narrow motif used, and the limits of each reference. No source text,
illustration, or audio has been copied into the original adventures.

- Four-part garden layouts and the role of water, plants, and architecture are
  documented in [The Persian Garden, UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1372/).
- Connected land and sea exchanges of goods, skills, and ideas are described in
  [About the Silk Roads, UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/silk-roads/about-silk-roads?hub=196704).
- A living regional bridge-making tradition and transmission of skills are
  documented in [Chinese wooden arch bridges, UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-design-and-practices-for-building-chinese-wooden-arch-bridges-02156).
- Wooden architecture and carpentry in a Karelian ensemble are documented in
  [Kizhi Pogost, UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/544/).
- A university educational edition of the traveling round-loaf tale is available
  in [Колобок, Moscow State University](https://skazki.irlc.msu.ru/russkie-narodnyie-skazki/kolobok).
  Bun’s plot, song, dialogue, and ending are original.
- The actual U.S. amendment text, including the Second Amendment, is available in
  [The Bill of Rights: A Transcription, National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/bill-of-rights-transcript).
  The archive display adventure concerns access to a complete verified source.

The United States chapter identifies subjects of the document and sends readers
to its text. It makes no present-day legal interpretation and contains no weapon
instruction. The fictional hall’s discussions leave room for different views.
Historical teaching, current legal questions, and product architecture belong
in their own clearly labeled resources.
