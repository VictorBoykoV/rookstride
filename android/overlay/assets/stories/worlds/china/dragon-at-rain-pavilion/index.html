Willow resting beneath the pavilion

Willow, a long gentle river dragon, curls beneath a pavilion as distant festival drums unsettle her and Rook asks to speak from a visible safe distance.

A dragon named Willow rested beneath a river pavilion during the festival. Whenever drums sounded nearby, her tail swept across the footpath. Some visitors called her unfriendly. Lin knew that Willow had asked for a quieter resting place before the festival began, but the message had been lost. Rook approached where she could see him and asked whether they could talk without the drums.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 05PAGE 2 / 4
The gardener considering the request

Rook carries a request between festival organizers and gardeners while Willow waits beside the pavilion, with a sheltered garden visible beyond the busy stage.

Willow explained that she loved the festival but could not sleep beside its loudest stage. The organizers had a sheltered garden farther from the speakers. They asked the gardeners whether it could host her and whether the visitors’ path should change. Rook carried the agreed messages instead of announcing that a dragon would simply move in. The gardeners proposed hours that worked for everyone.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 05PAGE 3 / 4
The agreed garden detour

Willow rests without disturbing plants in the sheltered garden as Lin marks a public detour and Rook places a child’s sleeping-cloud sign beside the entrance.

Lin marked the alternate footpath while stewards helped Willow reach her new resting place. The dragon folded herself carefully away from the beds. Rook left a clear sign with the gardeners’ chosen words, and a child added a picture of a sleeping cloud. Willow chuckled at it before settling down. Her tail stayed out of the path, and the afternoon concert continued.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 05PAGE 4 / 4
The reviewed Willow story

Willow enjoys quiet evening music beside the gardeners and Rook, with folded chairs and a reviewed story account resting near the pavilion.

In the evening Willow returned to listen to the softer music from a comfortable distance. She thanked the gardeners and helped carry their empty folding chairs with permission. Rook recorded a tale of neighbors adjusting a shared space, not of a bird taming a beast. Willow read the account and asked him to include that she had enjoyed the final song. He gave it a whole sentence.
