The festival lantern

Rook waits at a river-port information desk after mistaking a festival lantern sign for Mei’s workshop marker; Mei arrives with a blue umbrella.

The river city seemed to have a boat for every purpose. Rook followed a lantern symbol toward what he thought was Mei’s workshop and reached a festival storage room instead. The attendant kindly redirected him to the agreed meeting desk. Rook waited there, embarrassed but safe. Mei arrived carrying a blue umbrella. “You found a picture,” she said, “but not yet its meaning here.”

The checked route map

Mei and a shopkeeper help Rook compare a public route map with a simple verified sketch beside a busy printing street.

Mei walked him to the printing street and explained the route symbols used on the public map. Rook repeated the meeting place back to make sure he had understood. A shopkeeper offered directions with a sketch when words became difficult. Rook did not pretend he could read every notice. He thanked her and kept the verified sketch, including the desk where he could ask again.

The shared books

Mei reads Sima’s introduction at the workshop table while Rook sets down Leila’s credited notebook and shared books beside a kettle.

At the workshop, Mei opened Sima’s letter and recognized Leila’s little travel emblem on Rook’s notebook. They set the introductions beside a kettle and compared the books that had traveled between their libraries. Rook saw how much work had happened before his arrival. He was following friendships, not discovering a place that had somehow waited for him to put it on a map.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 01PAGE 4 / 4
Rook holding the printed card

Rook holds a newly printed contact-and-route card as festival lanterns glow above the river and Mei closes her workshop for the evening.

By evening Rook could find the workshop from the meeting desk without guessing. Mei let him print a small card with the agreed route and his own return contact. He left room for corrections as always. Outside, festival lanterns rose over the water for their actual purpose. Rook enjoyed them more now that he no longer needed every beautiful symbol to point toward himself.
