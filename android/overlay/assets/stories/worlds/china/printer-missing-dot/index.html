The unfinished route proof

Mei and Rook inspect a freshly printed festival route page at the workshop, with a small missing mark highlighted beside a closed-crossing sketch.

Mei was preparing a booklet for the river festival when Rook spotted a handsome route page. He offered to deliver the first stack at once. Mei asked him to read the route aloud first. A small mark had vanished during printing, changing a clear instruction into a confusing one. The page looked finished, but visitors might follow it toward the bridge’s closed maintenance entrance.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 03PAGE 2 / 4
Mei reviewing the paused stack

Rook gathers unissued booklets from a public tray while Mei marks a defective stack and Lin reviews the route wording with the workshop team.

They paused distribution and asked Lin to check the route wording. Rook counted the copies already placed in the public tray and collected them before anyone left with one. Mei marked the defective stack for recycling. Nobody blamed the apprentice who had first set the page. The error had passed several eyes, and their shared check had caught it while a correction was still easy.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 03PAGE 3 / 4
The revised route drawing

An independent reader follows the revised route from the information desk while Rook improves a confusing turn on the workshop’s proof copy.

The corrected booklet included the bridge’s opening times and a contact for updates. Mei asked a reader who had not worked on it to test the instructions from the meeting desk. He returned with a suggestion about a confusing turn. Rook revised the drawing, then tested it again. The handsome border remained, but the clearer route was what finally made the page ready.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 03PAGE 4 / 4
The checked festival booklet

A family follows the corrected festival booklet to an open river entrance while Rook dates his retained copy beside Mei’s printing table.

At the festival, a family used the booklet to reach the right entrance and thanked the printing team. Rook thought of all the people who would never hear about the missing mark. That was a satisfactory ending. He put one corrected copy in his atlas and wrote the date beside it. A message did not need to be dramatic; it needed to mean what its makers intended.
