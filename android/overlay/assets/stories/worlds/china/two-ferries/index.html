The two ferries

A route remains useful when its alternatives are clear

The ferries and landing route map

Passengers gather by Shen’s paused ferry timetable while Rook and Shen compare the downstream ferry’s landing and stairway on a public map.

Shen operated the small ferry linking the printing street to the opposite quay. One morning it needed maintenance, and passengers gathered beneath its timetable. Rook offered to announce that the larger ferry downstream would take everyone. Shen asked him to check its route and capacity first. It reached a different landing, and some passengers could not use the long stair from there.

The verified ferry and shuttle options

Shen and Rook post verified ferry and step-free shuttle options; passengers choose from the clear board rather than crowding around guesses.

Shen contacted the other ferry’s crew and the riverside shuttle. The team posted the checked options, including a step-free route and expected waiting times that could change. Rook stood beside the board to read it aloud for anyone who asked. A passenger chose to wait for the repair rather than take a longer journey. Rook respected that decision without calling it the wrong answer.

The traveler approaching the step-free shuttle

Rook guides a passenger to the marked shuttle stop as Shen updates the waiting-time board and the maintenance crew continues its checks.

The repair crew found that the small ferry needed more time. Shen updated the board instead of leaving the earlier estimate in place. The passenger who had chosen to wait changed her mind and took the shuttle. Rook walked her to its marked stop. Nothing about her earlier choice had been foolish; a new fact had made another route a better fit.

The River of Returning Lanterns / CHAPTER 08PAGE 4 / 4
The alternate routes left on the quay

Rook and Shen cross the river aboard the checked ferry while an alternate-route card remains on the quay notice board behind them.

By afternoon the ferry returned to service after its crew’s checks. Shen kept the alternate-route card on the notice board for future disruptions. Rook crossed with him and watched the two landings from the water. A city did not need every route to work every moment to care for its travelers. It needed honest updates and options people could actually use.
