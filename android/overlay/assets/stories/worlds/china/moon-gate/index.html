The circular doorway and private garden

Rook pauses at a circular garden doorway as Yu indicates the boundary between a public courtyard and a private meal beyond the next gate.

Rook saw a circular doorway framing a lovely small garden and stepped toward it with his atlas open. A caretaker named Yu welcomed him to the public courtyard but asked him to stop at the next gate. Beyond it, residents were having a private meal. The round opening made a beautiful view. It did not turn the people inside into part of a visitor’s exhibit.

The tested public garden plan

Yu and Rook review a public garden plan with residents and a first-time visitor at a courtyard desk, leaving the private gate closed.

Rook closed his sketchbook and apologized. Yu showed him a public garden plan with a suggested path and explained which spaces opened at different hours. He asked whether visitors needed clearer signs, and Rook described the mistake he had made without blaming the doorway. They invited a few neighbors to test the plan, including someone arriving for the first time.

Yu watching the agreed path

Rook sketches the empty public courtyard beside a small welcome notice while a resident passes privately beyond the frame and Yu watches the agreed path.

The neighbors chose a small notice that felt like a welcome rather than a scolding. It named the shared areas and offered a way to ask about special visits. Rook sketched only the empty public courtyard after receiving permission. A resident who did not want to appear in the drawing passed behind him without explanation. The drawing needed a garden, not every person in it.

Rook as an invited guest

Rook joins an invited community supper with his atlas closed in his satchel, framed by the round garden gate and warm evening lights.

Later Yu invited him to a community supper in the garden. Rook entered as a guest, with his notebook tucked away. The round gate looked exactly as beautiful as before, and the invitation made the evening richer. He left with the hosts’ chosen recipe note and no pictures of their private meal. A place could open generously because its people could also decide when to close.
