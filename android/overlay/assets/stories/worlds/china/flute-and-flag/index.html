The flute player

Rook begins too early at a riverside rehearsal as a musician’s tune echoes from a wall and a visitor asks about the gathering’s signal.

At a riverside rehearsal, a musician played a short tune to announce the storyteller’s arrival. Rook heard it twice as an echo returned from the opposite wall and began the introduction too early. The storyteller laughed and waited. Nearby, a visitor who did not hear the tune asked why everyone had moved at once. The welcome had reached some people and confused others.

The visible welcome flag

Visitors and organizers choose a visible flag and written start time while Rook practices raising the flag with the storyteller and musician.

The organizers asked the visitors what would help. They agreed on a visible flag and a clear written start time alongside the music. Rook offered to raise the flag, but practiced with the storyteller first so the movement matched the actual beginning. The musician kept the tune; adding another signal did not require taking someone’s art away.

The storyteller pausing

A steward helps Rook reset the wind-tugged flag from a safe position while the storyteller pauses beside a clearly visible page marker.

During the performance, a breeze tugged the flag out of place. Rook nearly chased it into the busy quay, then stopped and asked a steward for help. They reset it from a safe position. The storyteller paused long enough for everyone to find the next page. Nobody lost their place merely because one signal had failed for a moment.

An evening festival lantern over the river

Rook and the festival team add their own ideas to a printed signal note while music and lantern reflections drift across the evening river.

Afterward the visitor thanked the organizers for asking instead of guessing. Rook wrote a note beside his lantern signal from Lantern Harbor: Who can receive this, and how do we know? Mei printed the note for the festival team, who added their own ideas. The river still carried echoes, music, and wind. The welcome now had more than one way to arrive.
