The night-train curtain

A traveler can choose what to reveal

Dasha beside the travelers’ drawn curtain

Rook lowers his camera in a night-train carriage as one passenger draws a curtain and Dasha waits beside a closed travel notebook.

Dasha took Rook on a short train trip to an exchange library. He wanted a picture of the carriage full of warm faces against the snow. A passenger drew her curtain and asked not to appear. Rook lowered his camera at once. Another passenger preferred no photograph either. A shared carriage was a place to travel, not automatic permission to become part of his journal.

The secured lantern beside their boots

Rook and Dasha photograph their own boots, notebook, and secured lantern in an empty permitted common area of the train.

The crew showed him an empty common area where a picture would not expose other travelers. Rook photographed his own lantern and notebook there, then asked Dasha whether she wanted to appear. She chose a picture of their boots instead. They laughed at the oddly useful portrait. It showed a winter journey without borrowing anyone else’s face or revealing where they had slept.

The carefully prepared travel account

Rook and Dasha prepare a restrained travel account at the exchange library, including public service details and leaving fellow passengers unnamed.

At the exchange library, the host asked for a travel account. Rook described the journey without naming the passengers or the private stops they had mentioned. Dasha added the public train service information that visitors could verify themselves. Their account had enough detail to be useful and enough restraint to let strangers remain strangers. The host set it beside a map of agreed library visits.

The shared corridor beside a respected curtain

Rook and Dasha exchange a friendly corridor greeting before returning to separate train seats, with a closed camera and a respectfully drawn curtain nearby.

On the return train, the passenger who had drawn her curtain greeted them in the corridor. Rook did not treat the greeting as permission to take a picture after all. They shared a pleasant conversation, then went back to their separate seats. He remembered only what she agreed could become a story: sometimes a good journey includes a curtain that nobody tries to open.
