Misha, Anya, and Vera sharing distinct stories

Misha, Anya, and Vera share distinct stories in Dasha’s warm library beside an enclosed stove, while Rook revises an overly broad journal heading.

For Rook’s final library evening, Dasha invited neighbors to share their own stories. Misha brought a comic account of Bun. Anya brought a workshop memory. A grandmother named Vera told a new tale about a river that misplaced its reflection. Rook began writing One winter story from this country, then looked around the room. There were already three stories, and none spoke for everyone present.

The chosen story credits in Rook’s notebook

red Rook records chosen credits while Dasha makes a clearly labeled permitted summary and another neighbor’s private account remains unwritten.

He changed the heading to Stories shared at Dasha’s library on a winter evening. Vera approved and chose how her name should appear. One neighbor wanted her account to stay in the room, so Rook left it out of his notes. Another spoke in words Rook did not understand. With permission, Dasha summarized the meaning and marked the summary as her own, not the teller’s exact phrasing.

The Snowbound Library / CHAPTER 09PAGE 3 / 4
The separate garden and river story pages

Bun listens from a secure basket as neighbors choose among Rook’s garden and river accounts, with several distinct story pages laid open on a table.

The listeners asked questions without insisting on a single moral. Bun, sitting securely in his basket, preferred the funny ending to the useful one. Rook agreed that a story could be good without becoming advice. He showed the group his accounts from the garden and river worlds, where different friends had taught him the same restraint. Each person chose which page they wanted to hear.

The Snowbound Library / CHAPTER 09PAGE 4 / 4
The warm library light at the snowy gate

Rook returns borrowed winter clothes to Dasha’s loan basket and accepts a spare scarf before leaving the library’s glowing snowy gate.

After the last tale, Rook returned his borrowed boots and hat to the loan basket. Dasha wrapped a clean spare scarf for the coastal journey ahead. He asked if it should be returned. “Send a story instead, if you wish,” she said. Rook promised a story with real names only where their owners agreed. The warm library light followed him as far as the snowy gate.
