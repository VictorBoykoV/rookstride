Dasha welcoming Rook into the library

Dasha welcomes Rook into a timber library with a kettle, warm seats, books, and a visitor repairing a tablet at a side desk.

Snow brushed Rook’s scarf as Dasha led him from the station to a timber library. A sign offered warm seats to travelers during opening hours. Inside, a kettle hummed and someone repaired a modern tablet at a side desk. Rook had expected a place made only of old stories. Dasha smiled. “We read yesterday, today, and tomorrow here. Which seat would you like?”

Rook trying the borrowed boots and hat

Rook tries borrowed felt boots and an earflap hat with his red swept crest still visible while Dasha checks the fit beside a travelers’ loan basket.

Rook chose a place near the window and admitted that his bird feet were cold. Dasha found felt boots in the travelers’ loan basket and an earflap hat that kept his red face visible. She asked whether they fit before he wore them. They were useful borrowed clothes, not a costume he had to perform to belong. Rook thanked her and carefully noted where to return them.

The child’s wheeled-library drawing

Dasha reads Mei’s introduction as a child shows Rook an imaginative wheeled-library drawing near the exchange shelf holding Lin’s credited account.

Dasha read Mei’s letter and placed Lin’s permitted bridge account on the exchange shelf. A child asked if Rook had really come from a book. “That is how I remember it,” he answered. “I cannot tell you who drew the first page.” The child accepted that and showed him a drawing of a library with wheels. They imagined where such a library might travel.

The lamp beside Rook’s letter

Rook writes to Lumen from an arranged guest room, with borrowed boots drying safely by the door and the library lamp visible through the window.

When evening arrived, Dasha explained the library’s closing time and helped Rook find his arranged guest room. He did not mistake a warm public seat for a promise that every door would remain open forever. The guest-room window looked back toward the library lamp. Rook wrote to Lumen that he had found another good door, and a host who let him be both grateful and tired.
