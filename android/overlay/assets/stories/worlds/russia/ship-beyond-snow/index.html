Dasha’s sealed letter at the passenger desk

Rook confirms his passenger booking at a coastal terminal with Dasha’s letter for Jo secured in his satchel beside the sea-facing window.

The coastal passenger terminal smelled of wet coats and the sea. Dasha had sent a letter for Jo, an archivist in the Wide-Sky Commons. Rook checked his booking and route with the terminal staff, knowing that ships had their own arrangements beyond any map he drew. He put the letter securely beside Mei’s introduction and wondered how many friendships could fit inside one small satchel.

The fellow traveler lending a pen

Rook writes a carefully credited account in the terminal waiting area as a fellow traveler offers a pen during the announced boarding delay.

An announcement delayed boarding. Rook sat in the waiting area and wrote the story he had promised Dasha: the listening fox librarian, the loaf who chose to stop, and the travelers whose afternoon mattered together. He kept Vera’s permitted account distinct from Dasha’s summary. The pen ran out just before his own signature. A fellow traveler lent him another after he asked.

The Snowbound Library / CHAPTER 10PAGE 3 / 4
Rook securing his lantern aboard the ship

Rook boards and secures his lantern as instructed while the snowy coast recedes beyond the passenger deck window and earlier travel pages rest closed nearby.

When boarding began, he returned the pen and followed the crew’s directions. The snowbound shore slowly slipped behind the ship. Rook wanted to call the next sea completely new, but it held his earlier memory of Sima’s farewell and the rough first crossing. He secured the lantern in the approved place and let the water connect his stories without pretending the journeys were identical.

The far harbor under a broad open sky

Jo greets Rook at the agreed far-harbor desk with two cups of water, accepting Dasha’s letter beneath a broad open sky.

At the far harbor, Jo waited at the agreed meeting desk with a notebook and two empty cups. Rook handed over Dasha’s unopened letter and accepted a cup of water. Above the quay stretched a wide sky full of ordinary weather. “What have you brought?” Jo asked. Rook touched the satchel. “Stories with their owners’ names still attached, and enough blank paper for yours.”
