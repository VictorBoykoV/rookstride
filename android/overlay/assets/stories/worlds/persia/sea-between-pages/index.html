Sima's sealed introduction

Sima gives Rook a sealed introduction at a coastal quay while the captain indicates a safe place for the brass lantern aboard the ship.

At the coastal quay, Sima handed Rook a letter for Mei, a printer in the river world. “She helped us exchange books,” Sima explained. Rook asked what he should say about the garden. “Tell her what you experienced,” Sima said. “Leave room for what you did not.” The ship captain checked his ticket and showed him where to secure his lantern for the crossing.

Aster at the seaside farewell

Rook says farewell to Aster beside the gangway, checking credited drawings and permission notes in his satchel while the caravan prepares to return.

Aster had brought him as far as the port and would return with the caravan. Rook thanked her before boarding. Nima sent a drawing of the four garden paths with its keepers’ credits, and Leila sent permission to share her small travel emblem. The satchel felt full of names rather than souvenirs. Rook checked that each account still carried the choice of its maker.

The securely fastened cabin lantern

Rook and another traveler sit in a secure passenger cabin with the lantern fastened nearby while rain moves outside the window and a crew notice hangs clearly.

The ship’s first night was rough enough to make reading difficult. Rook stayed in the safe passenger area and listened to the crew’s announcements. Another traveler wanted reassurance that the ocean would become calm at once. Rook could offer company, but not that promise. They shared a story of a waiting bridge, then rested while the people responsible for the ship did their work.

The new river route behind Mei

Mei welcomes Rook at a busy river port, receiving Sima’s letter as green banks and river boats open beyond the passenger hall.

Morning revealed a river mouth lined with busy boats and green banks. Mei met the ship beside the customs hall and took Sima’s introduction. “You have come a long way,” she said. Rook looked back toward the water. “With a great many people’s help.” Mei smiled and led him toward the city. The garden remained in his atlas, and the river offered a fresh blank page.
