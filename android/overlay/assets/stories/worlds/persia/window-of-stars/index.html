Soraya's earlier observation notebooks

Soraya and Rook stand beside a covered telescope beneath clouds, then turn toward dated observation notebooks inside the tower room.

Soraya invited Rook back to the observatory before his ship departed. He expected a sky full of stars and found a roof full of clouds. Soraya did not pretend the telescope could see through everything. They checked the forecast, secured the equipment, and opened her older notebooks instead. Each page named when an observation had been made and what she had actually seen.

The honestly unobserved notebook corner

Soraya points to an honestly blank corner of a notebook while Rook moves his remembered star pattern onto a separate labeled sketch.

One drawing left a corner empty. Rook reached to fill it with a familiar star pattern from Lantern Harbor. Soraya stopped his pencil. That part of the sky had been hidden on the night of her observation. His remembered pattern might be beautiful, but it did not belong in her record as something she had seen. Rook added it to a separate sketch and labeled the difference.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 09PAGE 3 / 4
Zephyr showing an imaginative cloud picture

Zephyr presents a playful cloud drawing as Soraya and Rook arrange separate shelves for observed sky records and clearly labeled imaginative pictures.

Zephyr arrived with a cloud drawing of his own. It looked very little like the actual clouds, and he said so cheerfully. They made a small exhibit with two shelves: observations and imaginings. Visitors could enjoy both without confusing them. Rook added a fanciful harbor constellation shaped like Pip’s mailbag, while Soraya carefully kept her measured work on its own shelf.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 09PAGE 4 / 4
Rook looking through the prepared telescope

Rook views one bright point through Soraya’s prepared telescope as a small cloud gap opens above the garden tower and Zephyr watches quietly.

Just before closing, a gap appeared overhead. Soraya let Rook look through the properly set telescope and showed him one bright point. One was enough for the evening. He thanked her for the real glimpse and the honest clouds. In the morning he carried a permitted copy of her exhibit labels to his ship, ready to notice where his next view would also end.
