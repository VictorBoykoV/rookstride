The invitation Rook is revising

Rook revises a performance list beside Sima’s courtyard reading circle as Darya chooses a comfortable listener’s seat under the tree.

Sima planned a reading night beneath the courtyard tree. Rook made a list of everyone he thought should perform, then noticed Darya shrinking from her name. “I like listening,” she told him. “That does not mean I want the whole courtyard listening to me.” Rook crossed out the compulsory list and replaced it with an invitation people could accept, decline, or answer later.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 07PAGE 2 / 4
Darya helping Rook practice

Leila and Nima prepare their own short texts while Darya helps Rook practice a name; Sima arranges a quiet seat at the edge of the gathering.

Leila brought a poem she had written about a loom’s pause. Nima brought a short account of a repaired channel. Another neighbor asked for a quieter seat near the edge, and Sima made one. Rook offered to read the opening, but asked how his pronunciation sounded before the evening began. Darya helped him practice one name until it felt like a greeting instead of an obstacle.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 07PAGE 3 / 4
The reading-circle lantern

Leila reads beneath warm courtyard lamps while Rook listens; Darya privately hands Sima a folded note at the side of the gathering.

When Leila read, Rook listened without turning every image into a lesson about bridges. It was her poem, and some of its meaning belonged to her alone. During the pause, Darya quietly handed Sima a note for next month’s evening. She might like to read then. Nobody announced it. A choice could grow best when it did not become someone else’s celebration too soon.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 07PAGE 4 / 4
Rook recording only agreed contributions

The reading circle shares food after the poems while Rook records agreed credits and Sima keeps Darya’s note tucked safely in her notebook.

After the last reader, the neighbors shared bread and discussed what they had heard. Rook copied only the lines whose authors wanted to share them, using their chosen credits. Darya’s note stayed with Sima. The courtyard’s silence no longer felt like a gap waiting to be filled. It held listeners, pauses, and ideas that might find words on another evening.
