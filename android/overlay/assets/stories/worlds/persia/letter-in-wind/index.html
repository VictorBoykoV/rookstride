The wind-catching tower

Darya the courier stops red Rook below a wind-catching tower as a white envelope disappears into a shaded courtyard opening.

A gust lifted a letter from Darya’s courier tray and carried it into the shade beneath a tall ventilation tower. Rook raced after it, ready to fly up through an opening. Darya called him back. She knew which doors led to safe public spaces and which passages were for trained caretakers. Together they asked the tower keeper where the paper might have landed.

The recovered sealed envelope

The keeper returns a damp envelope to Darya and Rook; Darya compares its blurred outer address with her courier record at a shaded desk.

The keeper brought the letter from an accessible collection shelf. Its outer address had blurred where a drop of water touched it. Rook suggested opening it to find a name. Darya shook her head. “The words inside are not ours.” They checked her delivery record and contacted the sender, who could confirm the intended destination without revealing the private message.

The letter on Darya's secure tray

Rook steadies Darya’s tray as she carries a newly addressed outer envelope toward the courtyard library, with the tower keeper waving behind them.

The sender supplied a fresh outer envelope. Darya placed the unopened letter inside and asked the recipient where it should be delivered. He chose Sima’s library desk because he would be there that afternoon. Rook carried the tray beside Darya, holding it level through the next gust. The tower no longer seemed like an obstacle; it was simply a place whose keeper knew more than a visiting bird.

The Garden of Many Doors / CHAPTER 03PAGE 4 / 4
The recipient and Sima at the library

The recipient reads privately at Sima’s library while Darya thanks the tower keeper and Rook fits a cover over the courier tray.

At the library the recipient thanked them and opened his letter himself. Rook did not lean over his shoulder. Darya gave the keeper a secure tray cover for future deliveries, and he showed her where public notices belonged. The wind still moved through the courtyard. The new arrangement did not conquer it; it made a small, ordinary task less likely to go wrong.
