Sima the courtyard librarian

Sima welcomes Rook into a leafy courtyard library; a tortoise reads under a tree while Aster rests beyond the open gate.

Sima’s library opened onto a courtyard full of voices, flowerpots, and a patient tortoise reading beneath a tree. Rook looked for the grand door he assumed every library must have. Sima pointed to the ordinary one he had just used. “People come through that door every day,” she said. “A welcome need not be grand to be sincere.” Aster settled in the caravan yard nearby.

The blue curtain across a private doorway

Sima guides Rook toward shared reading tables and away from a curtained private doorway, with blue tiles and potted plants framing the courtyard.

Rook noticed a blue curtain across another doorway and moved toward it, hoping to explore. Sima gently asked him to wait. It led to a family room. She showed him the public reading tables, the garden path, and the desk where visitors could ask questions. Rook thanked her for explaining. An open library, he learned, did not make every neighboring room public.

Rook's new question sketch

Rook shares lunch with Sima and Nima beneath courtyard trees, writing a question beside a sketch of Lantern Harbor’s two bridges.

At lunch the neighbors asked what Lantern Harbor was like. Rook described Pip’s letters and the two bridges, then invited their questions. Nima, who cared for the garden’s water, asked about the stream beneath them. Rook knew less about that stream than he had imagined. He opened his notebook to a clean page and wrote a question to send Moss instead of making up an answer.

The agreed greeting on the travelers' shelf

Rook adds an agreed greeting to a courtyard travelers’ shelf while Sima and Nima point toward tomorrow’s garden walk beneath a warm evening lamp.

That evening Sima gave him a place at the travelers’ shelf. He put Lantern Harbor’s greeting there with Pip’s permission written on its envelope. Nima invited him to see the water channels in the morning. Rook had crossed a world without solving a single problem, yet his map had already changed: its newest mark was a table where a stranger had been allowed to become a guest.
