The private arrival ledger

Rook pauses before copying an arrival list onto Jo’s public travelers’ board as Sam questions the difference between a booking and an introduction.

Pip sent Rook a cheerful postcard addressed through Jo’s archive. Jo suggested a board where visitors could exchange public travel greetings. Rook almost copied the archive’s arrival list to start it. A traveler named Sam asked why a private booking had become a public introduction. Rook stopped immediately. The board needed volunteers, not names borrowed from people who had come for a different purpose.

Sam chooses a tree drawing

Visitors place chosen first names, a pen name, sketches, and permitted greetings on the voluntary board while other readers comfortably leave no entry.

They emptied the draft board and invited people to contribute what they wished. Sam chose only a first name and a sketch of a tree. Another traveler used a pen name; Jo explained that the board was for friendly accounts, not identity verification. Rook posted Lantern Harbor’s greeting with its existing permission. Some people chose no entry at all and remained just as welcome in the reading room.

The Wide-Sky Commons / CHAPTER 07PAGE 3 / 4
The child’s revised colorful entry

Jo, Rook, a child, and their guardian quietly revise a travelers’ board entry to use the archive’s moderated contact while preserving the child’s colorful drawing.

A child wanted to write a home address so strangers could send surprises. Jo helped the child involve their guardian and choose the archive’s moderated public contact instead. Rook did not use the moment to lecture the whole room. They quietly revised the entry together, and the child kept the colorful drawing. Thoughtful care could be a small conversation rather than a story about someone’s mistake.

The Wide-Sky Commons / CHAPTER 07PAGE 4 / 4
The voluntary travelers’ drawings

The voluntary board displays a varied set of agreed drawings and questions while Sam adds a thank-you message and Rook checks photography permissions.

By closing time the board held a forest, a harbor, a paper boat, and several inviting questions. Visitors could remove their entries or request corrections. Sam added a short message thanking the library for asking. Rook photographed only the entries whose makers agreed and left the rest out. A board could connect people without pretending that every traveler belonged on it.
