The actual passenger route

Rosa and Rook review a scheduled passenger route at an airfield information desk while a child and guardian look toward the public flight exhibition.

Rosa, a community pilot, invited Rook to a scheduled passenger flight toward Lantern Harbor’s region. A young visitor wanted to go too, imagining a plane as a magic shortcut to anywhere. Rosa explained the actual route, booking, and conditions, then suggested the airfield’s public exhibition with a guardian. Rook remembered his own first schedule. An exciting vehicle still followed a real plan.

Rook enjoys paper-plane play

Rook and the child make paper planes in an approved indoor exhibition activity area during an announced delay while Rosa’s official update remains visible nearby.

Weather delayed the flight, and Rosa directed passengers to the official updates. Rook stayed in the approved waiting area, telling the child about the library with wheels. Together they made paper planes for the exhibition’s indoor activity space. The child’s plane looped behind a chair. Rook’s landed at his own feet. They laughed without treating paper wings as practice for operating an aircraft.

The agreed paper-plane postcard

Rook boards the cleared passenger aircraft under crew direction with his satchel secured, leaving a permitted paper-plane postcard through the exhibition’s agreed contact.

When the flight was cleared through the responsible process, Rook followed the crew’s directions and secured his satchel. He sent the child a permitted postcard through the exhibition’s chosen contact, with a drawing of the very unsuccessful paper plane. Rosa did not promise an impossible view or an instant arrival. She promised the announcements and procedures that belonged to her actual job, then kept them.

Rook writes beside the passenger window

Rook looks from a passenger window at clouds above the sea and writes an honest note in his notebook, with the atlas kept safely on his lap.

From the window, Rook saw clouds like torn pages over the sea. He could not visit every place beneath them or solve every difficulty he imagined there. He opened his own notebook and wrote that down. The flight was carrying him toward home, not making him master of the sky. His next useful act would be a conversation with the people waiting at its end.
