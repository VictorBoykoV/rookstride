The signal for supper

Clear news can travel without exposing private lives

The closed private delivery record

Bea and Rook prepare a community-kitchen announcement while Jo prevents a list of individual family needs from becoming a public notice.

A delayed delivery left the community kitchen unsure how many suppers it could offer. Bea hosted a local information program and asked Rook to help collect updates. He began with a list of families who might need food. Jo stopped him before it reached the public board. A useful announcement needed serving times and contact options, not a display of people’s private needs.

Bea records the verified update

Bea records a plain-language meal update as Rook compares its written version with the kitchen coordinator’s confirmed times and private delivery contact.

The kitchen coordinator confirmed the available meals and arranged a private way to ask for delivery. Bea recorded the update in plain language and offered it in written form as well. Rook thought of the flute and flag by the river. A signal should have more than one usable form. He checked that the two versions said the same thing before they were shared.

The current supper notice

Rook updates a community notice with the coordinator’s new serving time while volunteers receive limited delivery details and Bea prepares a corrected broadcast.

A later delivery increased the number of meals, so the coordinator sent another verified update. Bea corrected the program’s notice instead of leaving the first estimate floating around. Rook helped mark the new time on the board. Volunteers received only the details needed for their assigned deliveries and returned their lists afterward. The kitchen stayed busy without turning gratitude or need into a public performance.

The supper-table lantern

Neighbors share supper beneath a table lantern while Bea and Rook keep the delivery records closed and the public notice shows only serving information.

By evening the shared tables were full, with private deliveries arranged through the agreed contact. Rook ate beside Bea and a volunteer whose name he had permission to include. He wrote a story about checked news and a meal, leaving out the recipients’ addresses. Nobody needed to become the subject of his journal to have supper. The lantern on the table lit people’s plates, not their private records.
