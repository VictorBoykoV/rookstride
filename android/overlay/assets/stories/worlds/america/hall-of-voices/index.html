Jo listens to the proposals

Rook holds a lantern sketch at a community hall meeting as neighbors propose daylight seating, quiet space, and affordable options around a shared table.

A community hall was planning its new reading corner. Rook arrived with Jo expecting everyone to agree that more lanterns would improve it. A neighbor wanted daylight seating. Another needed a quiet place away from the entrance. A third worried about cost. Rook looked at his lantern sketch and realized that arriving with a favorite answer had made him less ready to hear the actual question.

The visible proposal drawings

A facilitator records different proposals in large writing while Jo gives an accurate spoken summary and Rook adds his own suggestion among the others.

The facilitator gave each speaker time and made the proposals visible in large writing. A visitor asked for a spoken summary, which Jo provided without adding her opinion to it. Rook helped count the available chairs and wrote his own suggestion where it belonged, among the others. The meeting had room for disagreement because nobody’s preferred idea was allowed to become the only readable page.

The trial lantern

Two neighbors test different reading corners after a calm pause in the discussion, with Rook moving a trial lamp under the facilitator’s direction.

A sharp exchange interrupted the discussion. The facilitator paused it and asked both neighbors to name the practical need behind their words. One wanted light for small print; the other needed rest from noise. They did not suddenly become identical, but the group could work with both needs. Rook moved a trial lamp while the neighbors tested two different corners and reported what they noticed.

The Wide-Sky Commons / CHAPTER 02PAGE 4 / 4
Jo preserves the reviewed drawings

The hall displays a dated pilot plan and budget note while Jo preserves the proposals and Rook’s lantern lights one of several useful reading corners.

The group chose a small pilot arrangement and a date to review it. The budget note included what remained uncertain. Jo archived the decision with the earlier proposals, so future readers could see how it had been reached. Rook’s lantern was part of one corner, not the crown of the hall. He left pleased that a room could become more useful without declaring one neighbor the winner.
