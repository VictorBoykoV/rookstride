A welcome does not require a matching past

Jo welcomes Rook on the bench

Jo and Rook sit on a long public harbor bench as a neighbor moves her bag to make space, with the wide sky and passenger ships beyond.

Jo’s harbor had a long public bench facing the sky. Rook sat at one end with Dasha’s letter, unsure whether the other seats belonged to regular visitors. Jo explained that anyone could sit there during the harbor’s open hours. A neighbor moved her bag to make room. Nobody asked Rook to prove that his story began in the same place as theirs.

Room beside the usable bench

Rook notices a clearly marked repair section at one end of the bench while a traveler with a mobility aid joins the conversation in the open space beside it.

The bench needed a repair at its far end, marked by the harbor crew. Rook wanted to remove the barrier because it made the welcome look incomplete. Jo asked him to watch how visitors used the remaining seats and the clear space beside them. Someone arriving with a mobility aid had room to join the conversation. A marked limit could protect a welcome instead of contradicting it.

The open travel atlas

Jo reviews Rook’s permission notes and credited travel accounts at an archive table holding written pages and clearly labeled recordings.

Jo took him to the neighborhood archive, where accounts arrived in different languages and forms. Some were written, some recorded, and some shared only during visits. Rook offered his permission notes before his travel pages. Jo read the chosen credits carefully. “You have brought people with their choices intact,” she said. “That is a good way to enter a room full of stories.”

Rook enjoys the evening harbor

Rook places his own introduction in the archive, then sits with Jo on the usable part of the public bench beneath an open evening sky.

Before leaving the archive, Rook added his own short introduction. It named Lantern Harbor as home and described his uncertain beginning in the atlas. He did not pretend that Jo’s town had resolved the mystery. Outside, the repaired part of the bench was still closed. There was plenty of room to sit, ask another question, and enjoy a sky nobody at the table had made.
