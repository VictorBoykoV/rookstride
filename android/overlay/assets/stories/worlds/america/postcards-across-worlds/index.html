The distinct permitted travel accounts

Jo and Rook arrange credited accounts from garden, river, snow, and harbor worlds at an archive table while an unverified grand route sketch remains unfinished.

Before Rook’s departure, Jo spread his permitted travel accounts across the archive table. Garden paths, a river bridge, a snowy library, and the harbor’s two crossings sat side by side. Rook was tempted to draw one grand road joining every detail. Jo asked which parts had been checked as actual routes. He put the pencil down. A connection between stories was not a transport timetable.

Jo arranges the shared invitations

Rook and Jo compile dated invitations with chosen public contacts as new messages from Sima, Mei, and Dasha arrive beside Bun’s original short poem.

They made an atlas of invitations instead. Each entry named a public contact its owner had chosen, the date of the account, and the questions Rook still carried. Sima sent a new garden note. Mei sent a correction to a workshop opening time. Dasha sent Bun’s very short poem about staying home. Rook left all three in their makers’ distinct voices.

The Wide-Sky Commons / CHAPTER 09PAGE 3 / 4
The bridge picture and agreed invitation

Rook reads Lumen’s reply and sends a careful invitation to Lin through Mei while Jo checks the makers’ permission notes beside the atlas.

Lumen’s reply arrived last. The travelers’ shelf in Lantern Harbor had become a regular meeting place, and Moss wanted to discuss Lin’s bridge account with its makers’ permission. Rook asked Lin through Mei rather than assuming that one shared page invited every future use. Lin agreed to a conversation and chose what she would bring. The atlas began connecting people by their own decisions.

The Wide-Sky Commons / CHAPTER 09PAGE 4 / 4
The atlas leaves room for credits

Jo and Rook exchange agreed copies of the invitation atlas, opening the final credits and corrections page beneath the archive’s warm reading light.

Jo kept an agreed copy of the atlas and gave Rook the traveling one. Neither claimed to be complete. The last page named the people whose contributions had made it useful, with room for corrections and withdrawals. Rook thanked Jo for helping him resist a magnificent but false line. His worlds belonged together as neighbors in a book, each with its own doors, weather, work, and stories.
