The dragon at the gate

A keeper’s fear should not become everyone’s burden

Flint at the closed gate

Flint, a fictional gatekeeping dragon, blocks a town footbridge while Rook and Jo ask for the agreed public rules without revealing travelers’ private histories.

A dragon named Flint kept a town footbridge and feared that visitors might damage it. He began demanding a story of every traveler’s family before opening the gate. Some turned away rather than answer. Rook arrived with Jo and refused to tell another person’s private history. Instead they asked where the bridge’s agreed rules were posted and who was responsible for reviewing them.

Ada explains bridge care

Flint joins a public bridge review with Ada, the committee, and affected travelers while Rook contributes only his own experience at the meeting table.

The town’s bridge committee met with Flint and the affected travelers in the public hall. Flint admitted that he was afraid of being blamed for any damage. Ada explained the inspection and maintenance responsibilities already in place. Families described how his new demand had stopped ordinary journeys. Rook listened and added his own experience, leaving everyone else to choose what to say for themselves.

The checked public crossing notice

Flint removes his unnecessary private-history ledger from the gate while the committee posts agreed access rules and Ada reviews the actual safety notice.

The committee restored the agreed access rules and arranged support for the bridge keeper’s real maintenance concerns. Flint apologized without asking people to comfort him first. He removed his private-history book from the gate and left the public safety notices clear. Rook crossed only after the responsible keeper confirmed the bridge was open. A fairer rule did not mean ignoring the crossing’s actual condition.

Rook records the changed route

Flint gives ordinary directions beside the footbridge and a clearly marked maintenance detour while Rook records the corrected rule and apology in his atlas.

The next week Flint greeted travelers with directions instead of intrusive questions. He reported a damaged rail through the proper process and temporarily closed the crossing when instructed, with a marked alternate route. People could distinguish that needed closure from his earlier demand. Rook wrote no tale of slaying a monster. He wrote about a burden removed, an apology made, and a keeper learning the limits of his job.
