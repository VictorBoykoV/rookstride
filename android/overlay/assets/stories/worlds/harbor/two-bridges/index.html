Pip’s sealed letter

Pip shows Rook a sealed letter beside the near wooden bridge, with an island and unfinished stone crossing visible beyond the harbor water.

Pip showed Rook a sealed letter beside a harbor divided by a small island. A wooden bridge reached the island, and a stone bridge would carry the journey onward to Tess’s village. The far crossing still had scaffolding around it. “The first bridge is ready,” Pip said. Rook looked beyond its gate. One completed beginning could not yet give the letter a completed way to its destination.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 04PAGE 2 / 4
The near gate stays closed

Rook keeps the near wooden gate closed while Pip waits with his letter, and the unfinished far crossing remains visible across the harbor.

Rook rested a wing on the near gate and kept it closed. Pip waited with his letter while they arranged a check of the second crossing. “Then let us check the far bridge,” Rook said. Moss brought his tools, and he and Rook took the workboat by the safe route. They agreed that Pip would wait for their clear lantern signal before opening the first gate.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 04PAGE 3 / 4
Rook’s lantern on the checked far bridge

Moss and the crossing workers attend to the stone bridge while Rook walks its completed deck with his brass lantern to check the whole route.

At the stone bridge, Moss and the crossing’s workers finished the remaining work and checked its condition. Rook waited until the deck was ready, then walked the complete path with his lantern. He did not stop at the part that looked good from shore. Only after the far crossing had been checked from end to end did he raise the agreed light for Pip to see.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 04PAGE 4 / 4
The letter reaches Tess across both bridges

Pip hands the sealed letter to Tess on the far bank while Rook and Moss stand beside them and both complete bridges are visible behind.

Pip saw the gold signal, opened the near gate, and crossed both bridges. On the far bank, he placed the sealed letter in Tess’s paws. Rook and Moss stood beside them, with the complete route stretching back across the water. Tess thanked the courier and his companions. The letter had needed a good last crossing as well as a good first one, and all three friends had helped it arrive.
