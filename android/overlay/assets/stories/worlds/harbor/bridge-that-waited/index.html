Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 03PAGE 1 / 4
The gap in the damaged footbridge

Tess the otter with a green backpack meets Rook on the solid bank beside a damaged wooden bridge and a clear gap above the stream.

Tess the otter had a train to catch and a green backpack ready for the journey. At the stream, she found a gap in the wooden footbridge. Rook met her on the solid bank as rain darkened the hills. “Can we make it?” Tess asked. Rook studied the broken crossing before answering. Wanting to reach the station did not tell them whether this bridge could carry them.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 03PAGE 2 / 4
The loose plank checked from solid ground

Rook checks a loose bridge plank with his wing while remaining on the bank, and Tess waits safely beside him with her green backpack.

Rook checked the first loose plank from solid ground. The wood answered with a crack, and he drew his wing back. Tess waited beside him instead of stepping onto the damaged bridge. Rook folded his map and admitted they needed another beginning. A pause was disappointing when a train was waiting, but a broken plank was useful information. They turned toward the known bank path together.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 03PAGE 3 / 4
Boards carried along the safe bank

Rook, Tess, Pip, and Moss carry boards and red ribbon along the safe bank toward a marked alternative path beside the stream.

Pip and Moss joined them with boards and red ribbon. The neighbors knew a dry route along the bank where a short detour could be marked. Rook carried his share while Tess helped choose the turning toward the station. They worked with the people who knew the path, giving the next traveler clear markers instead of another guess. The original damaged bridge stayed out of their route.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 03PAGE 4 / 4
The marked detour beside the stream

Tess waves from the red-ribbon-marked detour while Rook, Pip, and Moss remain on the safe bank beside the stream and the damaged original bridge.

The marked detour let Tess continue by the longer way, and she reached her train. Before leaving, she waved from the safe path to the friends who had helped. Rook, Pip, and Moss stayed beside the stream to check the red ribbons for the next traveler. The first crossing had not become usable just because they wished it so. Another beginning had brought the journey to a good ending.
