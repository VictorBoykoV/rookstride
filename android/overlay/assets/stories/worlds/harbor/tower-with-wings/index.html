Mira's watchtower window

Mira looks from a watchtower window above a damaged stair, lowering a measured drawing to Rook rather than waiting silently for rescue.

Mira, the harbor’s wing designer, was stranded in the old watchtower when a storm damaged the stair. Rumor said she wanted a heroic rescue. Rook arrived below with a rope and discovered that Mira had already lowered a drawing. “Please find the tower keeper,” she called. “I need a proper inspection, a platform, and someone who can read these measurements.” Rook folded away his untested rope.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 08PAGE 2 / 4
The frightened young dragon

A keeper and repair crew review Mira’s plan in the courtyard while Rook speaks to a frightened small dragon beside a blocked equipment cart.

The keeper studied the plan and brought a trained repair crew. Rook carried messages between the safe courtyard and the workshop while Mira explained what had shifted inside. A frightened young dragon had sheltered in the tower shed during the storm. His tail blocked the crew’s cart. Rook asked where he would feel safe, and the dragon chose the empty boat house.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 08PAGE 3 / 4
Mira on the checked platform

Mira steps onto a checked platform with her drawings; Rook and the crew stand safely below while the dragon watches from an open boat house.

With the shed clear, the crew set up their checked platform. Mira descended with her drawings in a waterproof case. She thanked the crew first, then Rook for listening. The dragon peered from the boat house, worried that everyone blamed him for the broken stair. Mira showed him the storm damage. “You were in trouble too,” she said. “That is not the same as causing it.”

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 08PAGE 4 / 4
Rook recording shared credit

Mira presents a folding model wing in the restored tower courtyard; the dragon helps with boxes while Rook records everyone’s contributions.

At the tower’s reopening, Mira displayed a model wing that folded instead of fighting the wind. The dragon offered to carry empty boxes, and the keeper gave him a clear place to stand. Rook wrote the adventure as Mira’s plan, the crew’s skill, and a visitor’s useful errands. It made a fine rescue story, especially because its supposed hero had learned to share the title.
