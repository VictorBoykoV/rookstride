Wooden, stone, and metal crossing models

Rook and Moss show wooden, stone, and iron bridge models to Pip, the mouse neighbor, and Tess in a workshop filled with crossing sketches.

Moss set three little bridge models on the workshop table: wood, stone, and iron. Pip, a mouse neighbor, and Tess had three different journeys in mind. “Which is best?” Moss asked. Rook almost chose the grandest model, then looked at the travelers. A garden basket, a heavy mail cart, and a windy gorge were asking different questions. They would need to visit the places before answering.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 05PAGE 2 / 4
The short wooden garden crossing

Rook walks beside the mouse carrying garden vegetables across a short wooden footbridge with a handrail over the small stream.

The mouse’s garden route needed a few steady steps across a small stream. Rook accompanied her over the short wooden crossing as she carried vegetables home. Its modest shape fit that journey. Pip’s loaded mail cart asked for a different route, and the broad stone arch gave its wheels room. Rook began to see why the answer could change when the traveler, place, and load changed.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 05PAGE 3 / 4
The crossing drawing for the windy gorge

Rook and Tess remain on solid ground beside the windy gorge while studying a drawing of the planned metal crossing between the cliffs.

At the gorge, Tess’s scarf tugged in the wind while she and Rook studied a drawing from solid ground. They listened to the crossing’s experienced keepers before settling the plan. A properly designed and checked iron bridge later carried the long journey between the cliffs. Tess walked it steadily. The useful answer had come from this particular place and its requirements, rather than a material wearing a crown.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 05PAGE 4 / 4
Three crossings in their own places

All the friends gather around the map of three different crossings while Rook sets his brass lantern beside the workshop table and listens.

Back at the workshop, the friends gathered around a map showing all three bridges in their places. Rook set his lantern down and listened as each traveler described what had helped. Wood, stone, and iron had all found a useful role, with their own design and care. Moss smiled at the models he had first tried to rank. “Best for this journey,” Rook said, leaving every other journey its own question.
