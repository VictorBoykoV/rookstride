Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 09PAGE 1 / 4
The atlas leaf Rook is correcting

Rook erases an unverified mountain from a blank atlas leaf while Moss and Lumen sit at a library table with scattered route notes.

Rook laid his blank leaf on the library table and wrote Places I have not seen. Moss wanted to mark every shore before Rook left. Lumen asked who would check those marks. They stared at a magnificent mountain nobody present had ever visited. Rook erased it. “A blank space can be honest,” he said. “An invented answer can send someone the wrong way.”

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 09PAGE 2 / 4
Pip and Mira contributing their drawings

Friends place questions, a courier greeting, and Mira’s labeled wing sketch into a paper pocket attached to Rook’s travel leaf.

They made a pocket for accounts instead of filling the page with guesses. Fern contributed questions about gardens. Pip wrote a greeting for another courier. Mira added a sketch of her folding wing, labeled as her own experiment. Rook promised to ask before copying anything he encountered. The leaf became lighter to carry when it no longer pretended to know the whole world.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 09PAGE 3 / 4
The known route on the travel atlas

A visiting librarian marks a known route to a garden courtyard while Moss studies the many names credited along the atlas leaf’s edge.

A visitor from the Garden of Many Doors heard the discussion and offered Rook an introduction to Sima, a courtyard librarian. She drew the route she actually knew and left the rest for the caravan leader to explain. Moss noticed how many hands the map would need. “It will not be yours alone,” he said. Rook grinned. “That is why it might be useful.”

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 09PAGE 4 / 4
Rook's brass lantern

A new travelers’ shelf holds the atlas and an inviting blank notebook; Rook collects his brass lantern beneath a library’s open evening window.

They opened a small travelers’ shelf in Lantern Harbor. It held the atlas, agreed accounts, and a card saying that questions were welcome. The shelf’s first blank notebook belonged to nobody until someone chose to write. Rook set his lantern beside it for the evening, then took both leaf and lamp home. A journey had begun before he had traveled a single mile.
