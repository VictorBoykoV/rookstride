Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 02PAGE 1 / 4
Moss behind the crowded map stall

Rook finds Moss the hedgehog and a mouse customer at a map stall crowded with dangling signs, ribbons, banners, and hidden route drawings.

Moss made maps, but his market stall had become a forest of hanging signs. Ribbons brushed the counter, banners hid the drawings, and a mouse customer could scarcely see him. Rook stopped beside the crowded stall instead of squeezing through. “What would you like people to find here?” he asked. Moss peered around a dangling board. “A place where they can ask the way.”

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 02PAGE 2 / 4
Moss’s map at the crowded counter

Moss shows a large illustrated map through the stall’s hanging clutter while Rook listens and the mouse customer waits beside the counter.

Moss tried to show a map through the clutter while the customer waited. Rook asked which signs were useful and which had merely accumulated. They agreed to loosen the unwanted knots and give the counter room. Moss steadied the work, and Rook used his wings to reach the hanging ribbons. Taking things away was helpful only when they knew what they wanted to keep.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 02PAGE 3 / 4
The useful blue compass sign

Rook reaches toward the blue compass sign while Moss points out its purpose, with the stall’s remaining signs and map rolls around them.

Rook reached toward a small blue sign bearing a compass. Moss raised a paw before it disappeared with the others. “That one tells people what I do,” he explained. Rook paused, then gave the sign a clear place above the stall. A quieter market did not need to become a silent one. The useful compass could help someone find Moss’s maps and begin a conversation.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 02PAGE 4 / 4
The map handed to the mouse customer

Moss gives a folded map to the mouse at the tidy stall while Rook rests on its roof above the single blue compass sign.

At last the mouse could reach the counter and ask for directions. Moss handed over a folded map, hearing every question clearly. Rook rested on the stall roof above the single compass sign and watched the conversation unfold. The market still had colors, neighbors, and voices. What it had lost was the clutter that stood between a mapmaker and the person who needed his help.
