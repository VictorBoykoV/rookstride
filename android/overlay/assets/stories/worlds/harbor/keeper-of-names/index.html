The unfinished public map

Nell points at her upstairs room on Rook’s draft map in a public workshop; Rook pauses with his pencil lowered.

The harbor council asked Rook to draw a map of friendly houses where lost travelers could ask for directions. He began with places he knew from walking with Pip. Then a letter writer named Nell saw her small upstairs room marked on the draft. “I welcomed you once,” she said, “but I did not offer my door to every passing crowd.” Rook put his pencil down.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 07PAGE 2 / 4
Nell writing at her private desk

Rook consults shopkeepers while Nell writes route directions privately at a window; a public copy shows agreed gathering places instead of homes.

Rook apologized and erased Nell’s home from the public copy. He asked the other households one by one instead of assuming that kindness meant permission. Some offered their shops during opening hours. Others suggested the library or the quay office. Nell chose to help write clear directions from her desk, with her address kept off the page.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 07PAGE 3 / 4
The reviewed route map

Pip carries reviewed map copies while Nell adds her chosen first-name credit and Rook marks an accessible alternative beside a stairway.

Pip delivered the draft only to the people who had agreed to review it. They found an old opening time and a stairway that needed an alternative route. Nell’s careful directions solved both problems. When someone asked who had written them, Rook checked with her before answering. She signed them with her first name and smiled. That much sharing felt right to her.

Lantern Harbor / CHAPTER 07PAGE 4 / 4
The finished map and correction sketch

Neighbors gather around the finished map at the library; Nell shares tea while a correction note is visible on the map’s reverse.

The finished map welcomed strangers without sending them into private lives. On the back, Rook wrote how to request a correction or withdraw an offer. Nell brought tea to the library launch and chose to stay. “A door can be friendly and still have a lock,” she told him. Rook tucked his own copy into the atlas, grateful for the lesson he had almost missed.
