CHAPTER 01

The borrowed light

A little light goes further when you share it.

  1. 01

    A rabbit courier stands beside the dark harbor lighthouse with undelivered letters. Rook approaches with his lantern.

    The last letters were ready. The harbor light was not.

    “I can’t see the path,” said Pip.

  2. 02

    Pip holds an empty lantern. Rook listens beside the water.

    Rook stopped long enough to listen.

    “Then we’ll find it together.”

  3. 03

    Rook spreads a map under the glow of his brass lantern. Pip looks hopeful.

    One map. One lamp. Enough to begin.

    “The winding way is still a way.”

  4. 04

    Rook carries the lamp up a stone stairway, with Pip following toward the lighthouse.

    They took the steps one at a time.

    Pip followed the small circle of gold.

  5. 05

    At the lighthouse door, Rook gives his lit lantern to Pip.

    At the door, Rook passed the light on.

    “Keep it. Someone else may need you.”

  6. 06

    The lighthouse shines at sunrise. Pip delivers a letter while Rook waves from the bridge.

    By morning, the last letter had arrived.

    And the harbor had two guides.

CHAPTER 02

The noisy market

Keep the sign that helps. Clear the noise around it.

  1. 01

    Rook reaches a busy market stall hidden beneath hanging signs. A hedgehog mapmaker and a mouse customer struggle to see each other.

    Moss made maps. Nobody could find them.

    His stall had become a forest of signs.

  2. 02

    Moss tries to show a map through the tangled ribbons while Rook listens.

    Rook asked what Moss wanted to keep.

    “A place where people can ask the way.”

  3. 03

    Rook unties excess banners while Moss steadies a small ladder.

    They loosened a knot, then another.

    Slowly, the counter came back into view.

  4. 04

    Rook reaches for a blue compass sign. Moss points out that this sign is useful.

    One small blue sign caught Moss’s eye.

    “That one tells people what I do.”

  5. 05

    Rook and Moss put the compass sign above the tidy, open stall.

    So they gave it room to be seen.

    A quieter stall still had a voice.

  6. 06

    Moss hands a folded map to the mouse. Rook rests on the roof above the single compass sign.

    At last, a customer asked a question.

    And Moss could hear every word.

CHAPTER 03

The bridge that waited

Beginning again is a way forward, too.

  1. 01

    An otter traveler with a green backpack meets Rook beside a damaged wooden footbridge over a stream.

    Tess had a train to catch. The bridge had a gap.

    “Can we make it?” she asked.

  2. 02

    Rook checks a loose bridge plank with his wing while Tess waits on solid ground.

    Rook checked the first loose plank.

    The wood answered with a crack.

  3. 03

    Rook and Tess turn back toward a stone path marked on his map.

    He folded the map and stepped back.

    “We need another beginning.”

  4. 04

    Rook, Tess and two harbor neighbors carry small boards and a red ribbon along a safe bank.

    They found neighbors, boards and a dry bank.

    Four pairs of feet made a new path.

  5. 05

    The neighbors mark a short safe detour with red ribbons and a small boardwalk.

    A red ribbon marked each turning.

    No traveler would have to guess.

  6. 06

    Tess waves from the safe detour. Rook and the neighbors sit by the stream as sunlight returns.

    Tess made her train by the longer way.

    Rook stayed to mark the next turn.

CHAPTER 04

The two bridges

Check the far bridge before opening the near gate.

  1. 01

    Pip shows Rook a sealed letter beside a harbor divided by an island. A wooden bridge reaches the island; a distant stone bridge is unfinished.

    One letter. Two bridges. A village waiting beyond them.

    “The first bridge is ready,” said Pip.

  2. 02

    Rook keeps the near wooden bridge's gate closed while Pip waits. The unfinished far bridge remains visible across the water.

    Rook rested a wing on the closed gate.

    “Then let’s check the second.”

  3. 03

    Rook and Moss take a small workboat carrying tools toward the unfinished stone bridge.

    Moss brought his tools. Rook brought the light.

    They took the workboat by the safe way.

  4. 04

    Moss works on the completed stone bridge while Rook walks its deck with his lantern, checking the path.

    A final stone. A careful walk from end to end.

    Only then did Rook lift his lamp.

  5. 05

    Rook signals with his raised lantern from the far shore. Pip opens the near wooden gate and prepares to cross.

    Across the water, a small gold signal shone.

    Pip opened the gate.

  6. 06

    Pip delivers the letter to Tess on the far bank. Rook and Moss stand beside him; both bridges are complete behind them.

    The letter crossed both bridges and found Tess.

    A good first step had needed a good last one.

CHAPTER 05

The right material

Choose the bridge that fits the journey.

  1. 01

    Rook and Moss show models of wooden, stone and iron bridges to Pip, a mouse neighbor and Tess in a workshop.

    Three neighbors. Three journeys. Three little bridges.

    “Which is best?” asked Moss.

  2. 02

    Rook helps the mouse carry garden vegetables over a short wooden footbridge with a handrail.

    The garden needed a few quiet steps over a stream.

    Wood made a simple way home.

  3. 03

    Rook and Moss help Pip push a heavy mail cart over a broad stone arch bridge beside the harbor.

    Pip’s cart brought the weight of a hundred letters.

    The stone arch gave every wheel room.

  4. 04

    Rook and Tess inspect a windy gorge from solid ground, holding a drawing of a metal bridge.

    At the gorge, Tess’s scarf tugged toward the wind.

    They listened before they drew.

  5. 05

    Tess and Rook walk across a completed iron truss bridge with railings spanning the deep gorge.

    Iron beams held the long crossing between the cliffs.

    Tess kept walking, one steady step at a time.

  6. 06

    All the friends gather around a map showing the three bridges in their different places. Rook sets his lamp down.

    Back at the table, every bridge had found its place.

    “Best for this journey,” said Rook.
